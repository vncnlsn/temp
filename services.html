Title & Ownership

APS researches surface and mineral ownership at any stage of a project, from early feasibility through active permitting and acquisition. Preliminary research can reveal who controls the land, how ownership is divided, where access may be difficult, and how many interests will need to be addressed. That information helps define project scope, permitting strategy, staffing, and cost before significant resources are committed.

Ownership records can quickly become complicated. Surface and mineral estates may have separated generations ago, interests may be divided among multiple heirs, and federal, state, or tribal lands can involve separate records and approval requirements. APS traces and reconciles ownership through public sources, courthouses, and government archives, then maintains the resulting ownership information for use throughout GIS mapping, permitting, acquisition, and field operations.

GIS Services

APS builds comprehensive project maps and databases that connect ownership and title information to the land itself. Surface and mineral ownership, legal descriptions, topography, infrastructure, project boundaries, and other relevant data are organized geographically for planning, permitting, acquisition, and field use.

Surface and mineral interests don’t always follow the same boundaries. Historical legal descriptions may not align cleanly with current parcels, and a change in project alignment can introduce an entirely different set of affected properties. APS reconciles those changes in the mapping and maintains the supporting project data as the work develops.

Seismic Permitting

Seismic permitting has been at the core of APS’s work for more than 45 years. We manage the land access and permitting required for 2D and 3D seismic surveys, microseismic, Vertical Seismic Profile (VSP), and other geophysical field programs.

Every property presents unique conditions. Access routes, crops and livestock, fences, water wells, existing infrastructure, environmental restrictions, compensation, and potential damages can all affect where and how survey crews operate. APS personally contacts landowners to explain who we represent, what the survey involves, what access is being requested, and how the property will be used. We negotiate and document those conditions, then oversee their enforcement in the field to protect all parties.

Right-of-Way Acquisition

APS secures linear property rights for pipelines, electric transmission, utilities, telecommunications, roads, and other infrastructure. We verify corridor ownership and existing rights, identify affected tracts and encumbrances, personally contact landowners, negotiate compensation and property terms, and execute easement and access agreements.

ROW acquisition develops alongside the route itself. Requirements vary tract by tract, and a rejected agreement, divided interest, existing easement, utility conflict, or physical constraint can change an alignment after acquisition is already underway. A reroute can introduce new parcels, owners, title, mapping, and negotiations into the project. APS identifies these conflicts early and keeps ownership, GIS, acquisition status, and route changes synchronized as the corridor develops.
